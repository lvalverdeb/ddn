# Re-audit — Tasks 10–13, after the fixes

**Date:** 21 September 2026  
**Commit:** the tip of the Task 10–13 series, suite green at 798  
**Baseline:** `conformance-2026-09-19.md`, written at `96b5f2f` against spec v0.12  
**Method:** 10 agents — 5 verifying one area each, 5 refuting every FIXED claim. 22 claims were downgraded on refutation.

> A row is **FIXED** only where the code was opened, the test was opened, and the test fails when the fix is reverted. A fix with no discriminating test is **STILL_OPEN** — the behaviour may be right, but nothing holds it there.

## Verdict

**Tasks 10–13 did the work they claimed, and the re-audit is worth having anyway.**
Fourteen rows are held by a test that fails when reverted. Thirty-nine are
`PARTIAL` — the code is right and the test is weaker than the claim. But seven
rows **regressed**, five of them introduced by the fixes themselves, and two of
those were serious enough to fix on the spot (see the closing section).

The pattern across the `STILL_OPEN` rows is one thing, repeated: **a fix landed
in three places and a test was written for one of them.** §8's costs reach all
three vehicle builders and only `contract`'s is held — zeroing the binding in
`returns/run.py` leaves the suite green at 796. The prose counts were corrected
in four files and nothing holds any of them, so all four can drift back
silently.

The most uncomfortable finding is that a guard written in this series was
**defeated during the re-audit**: setting `contract.PRIZE_SCALE = 250` — a
module-local invented cost ratio diverging from the registry — leaves the suite
green, because 100 is not in the guard's named subset and because
`test_contract.py` computes its expectation as `S = contract.PRIZE_SCALE`, a
constant compared against itself. That is the exact failure mode this
repository's own docstrings warn about, in the same file.
