# Proposed: §10's D2–D6 arithmetic pools capacity that cannot be pooled

**Status:** proposal. Nothing here has been applied: §10 still pools D2–D6's
bikes against their pooled demand. Written against **Draft v0.15** and
re-checked against **Draft v0.17**, where the defect below is unchanged. v0.16
and v0.17 went to other proposals while this waited, so the target — and this
file's name — is now **v0.18**.
**Date:** 22 September 2026
**Found by:** building e2e-3's acceptance row C3 (Task 15).

---

## The defect

§10 computes the morning's unassigned envelopes like this:

> D2–D6 48 bikes × 25 = 1,200 vs 1,330 → 130 unassigned across the five.
> Total unassigned 150; dispatched 2,950.

That treats the five depots' bikes as one pool. They are not. §10's own
allocation fixes each depot's fleet before the day starts — HUB 48, D1 24,
**D2 18, D3 14, D4 8, D5 6, D6 2** — and a bike at D2 cannot serve a stop from
D6. Per depot, on §10's own figures:

| Depot | Pool | Bikes | Capacity | Slack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D2 | 412 | 18 | 450 | **+38** |
| D3 | 348 | 14 | 350 | **+2** |
| D4 | 253 | 8 | 200 | −53 |
| D5 | 203 | 6 | 150 | −53 |
| D6 | 114 | 2 | 50 | −64 |

The three shortfalls are **170**, not 130. The difference is exactly the 40
envelopes of slack sitting at D2 and D3, which no vehicle can carry to D4, D5
or D6.

**This is not a rounding question.** `docs/solver-capabilities.md` §2 answers
*flexible vehicle-to-depot assignment* with **No**, and §4.2 is a two-stage
problem *because* of it: "a vehicle's depot is fixed before the solve; it is
not a decision variable". A figure that lets D2's spare riders serve D6 is
describing an operation this one is not.

It also propagates. §10's next bullet reads "Tomorrow's delivery pool: 4,550
positioned today + **150 unassigned** + 120 postponed = 4,820", so the total
and tomorrow's pool are both 40 low.

## Where it came from

§14 records v0.13 as "Corrections from conformance audit: §10 arithmetic closed
(10 discrepancies; **D2–D6 leave 130 unassigned**…)". So this line was written
*by* an arithmetic review and the pooling survived it — which is worth saying,
because it is the second time a §10 figure has been checked against a
restatement of itself rather than against the allocation it follows from.

## Proposed change

```diff
-- Delivery (today's routes, on the morning pool of 3,100 = HUB 1,150; D1 620; D2–D6 1,330): HUB 48 bikes × 25 = 1,200 vs 1,150 → all assigned. D1 24 × 25 = 600 vs 620 → 20 unassigned. D2–D6 48 bikes × 25 = 1,200 vs 1,330 → 130 unassigned across the five. Total unassigned 150; dispatched 2,950.
+- Delivery (today's routes, on the morning pool of 3,100 = HUB 1,150; D1 620; D2–D6 1,330): capacity is per facility, because §4.2 fixes a vehicle's depot before the solve (§12 Q5). HUB 48 × 25 = 1,200 vs 1,150 → all assigned. D1 24 × 25 = 600 vs 620 → 20 unassigned. D2 18 × 25 = 450 vs 412, D3 14 × 25 = 350 vs 348 → none; D4 8 × 25 = 200 vs 253 → 53; D5 6 × 25 = 150 vs 203 → 53; D6 2 × 25 = 50 vs 114 → 64. D2–D6 leave 170; the 40 envelopes of spare capacity at D2 and D3 cannot reach the depots that are short. Total unassigned 190; dispatched 2,910.
```

and, following from it:

```diff
-- Tomorrow's delivery pool: 4,550 positioned today + 150 unassigned + 120 postponed = 4,820.
+- Tomorrow's delivery pool: 4,550 positioned today + 190 unassigned + 120 postponed = 4,860.
```

**2,910 is what the simulator already answers.** `run_day` on §10's stated
morning dispatches 2,910 and leaves 190 unassigned — measured, and pinned in
`tests/test_simulation.py`. The document and the code disagree only here.

## What it changes in this repository

- `tests/fixtures/peak_day.py` carries §10's published figures and would move
  with it.
- e2e-3's row **C3** reads "130 unassigned across the five, each depot's own
  lowest-priority". The two clauses contradict each other: leaving each
  depot's own lowest-priority gives 170. `tests/e2e/test_slice_3_depot_delivery.py`
  asserts 170, reproduces §10's 130 beside it, and names this file — so the
  test states the disagreement rather than encoding either number as settled.

## A second question this raises, which is operations' and not arithmetic

D6 has 2 bikes for 114 envelopes and leaves 64 — more than half its pool,
every day, structurally. §4.2 allocates "by demand", and an allocation that
leaves one depot at 44% service while another has 38 envelopes of slack is
either the intended trade-off or a defect in the allocation rather than in the
arithmetic. §8.3's capacity finding does not cover it. Worth a look before the
figures above are treated as the operation's target state.
